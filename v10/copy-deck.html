1 · Hero — the pair, developed

The founder asked to keep the “Stories on screen. / Systems behind them.” direction but develop it, and to give the two halves real typographic contrast rather than two lines at the same size. In every direction below, the first line is the studio voice (Fraunces, light, large) and the second is the work voice (Archivo, condensed, black, uppercase) — the two halves of the company made visible in the type itself. The explicit descriptor sits above, readable at first paint.

Direction A — developed from the founder's own line in the build

Lion Creatives — Creative and Technology Agency

Stories on screen. Systems behind them.

We direct commercial film and photography — and design and build the platforms they run on. Maryland-based, on location and remote.

Why: it keeps the line the founder liked and fixes what was wrong with it — the halves no longer look the same. “Systems behind them” now reads as engineering because it is set as engineering. Both halves are literally true.

Direction B — the two crafts, named

Lion Creatives — Creative and Technology Agency

We direct the film. We build the platform.

Commercial film, photography, and the web systems they run on — from one team in Maryland.

Why: the plainest possible statement of the two services, with no interpretation required. Risk: less memorable as language; it explains rather than positions.

Direction C — the buyer's project, not our craft

Lion Creatives — Creative and Technology Agency

Your film. Your platform. One team.

From a 4K commercial to the site that sells it — directed, designed and shipped by Lion Creatives.

Why: speaks to what the client is buying. Strongest for conversion, least distinctive as language.

Direction D — editorial

Lion Creatives — Creative and Technology Agency

The camera, then the machine it lives on.

Lion Creatives directs film and photography, then engineers where the work lives.

Why: the most ownable image. Risk: it asks the visitor to interpret, and the founder has been clear that clarity comes first.

3 · The closing line — five candidates

The founder scored the old finale 0/10 and asked whether “Let’s make something unmistakable.” is worth keeping at all. It is not assumed to survive. The build currently runs candidate 1 as a working choice so the new finale can be judged with real type in it; nothing is locked.

Candidate 1 in the build

Directed
and built by one team.

Why: it is the whole company in five words, it is literally true, and it closes the loop opened by the hero (“Stories on screen. / Systems behind them.”) without repeating it. Three lines give the finale a real rag.

Candidate 2

Bring us
the hard one.

Why: confident and human, and it earns the swagger because the visitor has just seen the work. Risk: it filters out smaller briefs the studio may want.

Candidate 3

Film, photographs,
and the software
behind them.

Why: the safest and most literal. Deliberately reprises the hero. Risk: it explains rather than closes, and it is long for a finale.

Candidate 4

The next one
is yours.

Why: hands the frame to the visitor in five words and lands directly on the CTA. Risk: it echoes the conversion scene’s “yours.” one scene earlier.

Candidate 5 — the incumbent

Let’s make something
unmistakable.

Why it is listed last: “unmistakable” is a claim about work the visitor has just judged for themselves, and “let’s make something” is the most common closing construction in the sector. Kept here only so the founder can compare directly.

4 · What is deliberately not said

No customer counts, ratings, uptime figures or testimonials — including the client's own, which appear on their live site but are theirs, not ours. No invented production credits: we say we were hired to film the proposal and the ceremony, because that is what happened. No capacity or turnaround promises. No awards. No philosophy addressed at the visitor.
