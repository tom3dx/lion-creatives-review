Studio — Maryland, USA

The people who shoot and the people who ship are one team.

Lion Creatives is a creative and technology agency. We direct the film and we write the code, so there is no seam in the middle for the work to fall through — and nobody to point at when it does.

01How we work

Operating rules, not adjectives.

Every studio calls itself meticulous. That is not a claim you can check before you sign. These are — so check them, and hold us to them once the work starts.

04

Projects per quarter — the studio ceiling

Sixteen projects a year, not sixty. Capacity is the constraint this studio is built around, because the alternative is a layer of people you never met doing the work you were sold.

When a quarter is full we give you the next date. We do not quietly staff around it.

01

One organisation, no handoff

The director and the lead engineer sit in the same weekly review. There is no partner agency to escalate to, because the second half of the job is ours as well.

02

You meet the people who do the work

The team on the first call is the team on the shoot and the team in the pull request. No pitch crew, no substitution after signature.

03

Scope in writing before making starts

A written scope, a named producer, real dates. What is out of scope is written as plainly as what is in it, and changes are priced when they are asked for rather than discovered at the invoice.

04

Work in progress, weekly

Dailies, cuts, staging links. You see the work while it can still be changed, not as a reveal at the end when changing it costs a re-shoot.

05

Handover is part of the build

Written documentation and a recorded walkthrough ship with the work, so whoever inherits it is not calling us to ask where things are.

02The people

Roles, so you know who is actually on it.

A small senior team. Each role below owns a decision rather than attending a meeting about it, and each one is someone you will meet before you commit.

Placeholder frames

Team photography is pending and will be shot by this studio. Stock portraits will not be substituted. A purchased photograph of people who do not work here is one of the fastest ways to lose a serious client, and a grey frame is the more honest placeholder.

Founder / Creative Director

Owns the idea and the argument behind it. Directs the films, art-directs the stills, and is in the room for the technical decisions that shape both.

Director of Photography

Owns the frame. Lighting, lensing, camera movement, and holding the look from the first setup through to the grade.

Lead Engineer

Owns what ships. Architecture, code review, performance budgets, accessibility, and the deploy itself — including the one at eleven at night on launch week.

Producer

Owns the schedule and the budget. Crew, locations, permits, vendors, and the dates everyone else is working to.

03Method

Five moves. Each one leaves something in your hands.

Process diagrams are easy to draw and impossible to check. So here is each stage described by what you actually receive at the end of it.

01

Discovery

What you get

A written read of the problem before anyone opens a camera or an editor. Stakeholder interviews, an audit of what already exists, and one page stating the problem in language you can forward to your board without translating it.

02

Strategy

What you get

A position you can say out loud, a message hierarchy, and a decision about what gets made. What we are deliberately not making is written down too — that list is usually the more valuable half.

03

Creation

What you get

The shoot, the edit, the identity, the build — run as one project rather than four. Dailies, cuts and a staging URL arrive weekly, so feedback lands while it is still cheap to act on.

04

Launch

What you get

A dated launch plan with named owners, a staging environment you sign off before anything is public, and a rollback path rehearsed in advance for the day it goes sideways.

05

Growth

What you get

Measurement wired in before launch instead of bolted on after, and a standing review where we look at what the work is doing and change it. Optional and scoped separately — some projects should end at launch, and we will say so.

04Where we are
