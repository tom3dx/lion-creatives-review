01The work

Six projects. Not an archive.

A catalogue measures how much work a studio has taken on. A curation shows how it thinks. Each of the six below is here because it argues something different — about direction, about engineering, or about what happens when the same team does both.

They are ordered, not ranked. Read straight through and the argument builds: one project where the two disciplines are fused, then the pillars on their own terms, then two more where they meet again.

Film & Video · Photography · Brand & Digital · Technology

01 — Film & Video · Technology

A brand film and the platform it launched on

Direction and engineering ran on one schedule instead of two. The site was built around the cut rather than waiting for it, so the campaign and the product it pointed at went live on the same day — and look like they came from the same room, because they did.

02Single-discipline work

Two projects that only needed one pillar.

Integration is the differentiator, not the requirement. Plenty of good work is one discipline done properly — and it should be judged on that, not on how many departments touched it.
02 — Photography

A photography set built on one lighting idea

A season of product and lifestyle images that hold together because they were lit as a single argument rather than assembled from a shot list. The client stopped re-shooting between drops.

Marks, type, grid and motion delivered with the reasoning attached, not just the files. The point of a system is that it still looks deliberate the month after the studio leaves.

Why only six

We would rather defend six projects line by line than list sixty and hope nobody asks.

Everything on this page was directed and engineered inside one organisation. There is no partner studio in the credits you never spoke to, and no stage of the work that got handed somewhere else to finish.

03Where the pillars meet

The interesting problems sit between departments.

An AI product is a design problem before it is a modelling problem. A campaign that needs film and stills is a scheduling problem before it is a creative one.

Both of the next two exist because nobody had to negotiate across a boundary to solve them.
